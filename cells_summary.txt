## 1. The game in one minute

<div class="kg-prose"><div class="lead">Kaggriculture is a <b>two-player farming economy</b>. One season is <b>30 days × 24 turns = 720 turns</b>. The player with the higher terminal bank wins. Coin margin is useful for diagnosis, but strategy promotion is decided by <b>wins, losses, and ties</b>.</div></div>

<div class="kg-strip">
  <div class="kg-mini"><b>Capital</b><small>land · animals · seeds</small></div>
  <div class="kg-mini"><b>Operations</b><small>workers · routes · timing</small></div>
  <div class="kg-mini"><b>Biology</b><small>growth · water · fertilizer</small></div>
  <div class="kg-mini"><b>Market</b><small>shared prices · town demand</small></div>
  <div class="kg-mini"><b>Finish</b><small>cash in the bank at turn 720</small></div>
</div>

<div class="kg-note gold"><div class="title">The key operating insight</div><div class="body">A high spot price can still be a bad decision if the crop takes too long, consumes scarce labor, or crashes after repeated sales. Good play is about <b>economic timing</b>, not just expensive items.</div></div>


<div class="kg-note teal"><div class="title">Current town-demand regime</div><div class="body">The Town Center now removes one of each non-fertilizer product <b>once per day at a flat rate</b>. Shops unlock <b>with replacement</b>, so duplicate shop instances can concentrate demand on a small set of products. The controller therefore reads the live shop list instead of assuming a balanced town.</div></div>

## 3. Opening economy EDA

<div class="kg-prose"><div class="lead">A high opening price is only a clue. The real decision must combine price, growth time, worker travel, feed, storage, and the price erosion caused by selling into a shared market.</div></div>

=== CELL 10 ===
## 4. Architecture: keep the route, adapt the premium queue lead

<div class="kg-prose"><div class="lead">The season plan and the market-timing layer solve different problems. The route selector decides <b>what the farm becomes</b>; the queue lead decides <b>when premium inventory reaches the shared market</b>. This separation lets the agent react to competitive pricing without rewriting labor, livestock, or crop logistics every turn.</div></div>

<div class="kg-flow">
  <div class="kg-flow-item"><div class="kg-flow-badge">1</div><div><b>OPENING SIGNAL</b><span>read the first unlocked shops</span></div></div>
  <div class="kg-flow-arrow">→</div>
  <div class="kg-flow-item"><div class="kg-flow-badge">2</div><div><b>SEASON ROUTE</b><span>choose one complete farm trajectory</span></div></div>
  <div class="kg-flow-arrow">→</div>
  <div class="kg-flow-item"><div class="kg-flow-badge">3</div><div><b>QUEUE LEAD</b><span>one-turn or two-turn premium timing</span></div></div>
  <div class="kg-flow-arrow">→</div>
  <div class="kg-flow-item"><div class="kg-flow-badge">4</div><div><b>GUARDS</b><span>weeds · storage · sell slots · closeout</span></div></div>
</div>

<div class="kg-note"><div class="title">Why timing matters in a shared market</div><div class="body">Selling inventory changes the state seen by later sellers. A strong production route can therefore lose value simply by joining the queue too late. This controller preserves the production plan and changes only a bounded premium-sale window, then removes the corresponding scheduled sale later so the shift does not double-sell inventory.</div></div>

=== CELL 12 ===
## 6. Four practical control layers

<div class="kg-grid">
  <div class="kg-card green"><div class="eyebrow">A · Coherent production routes</div><b>Let persistent demand choose the farm.</b><br><span>Yarn position, milk-supporting shops, and the fallback regime select a complete season trajectory instead of repeatedly redesigning the production mix.</span></div>
  <div class="kg-card gold"><div class="eyebrow">B · Premium queue lead</div><b>Protect the highest-leverage sale windows.</b><br><span>For strawberry, melon, milk, and wool, the controller inspects the route's upcoming sale and can move a bounded batch earlier when the inventory is already available.</span></div>
  <div class="kg-card violet"><div class="eyebrow">C · Demand-aware horizon</div><b>Use the aggressive lead only where it stays stable.</b><br><span>Broad crop and milk regimes use a two-turn lead. Yarn and bakery openings keep a one-turn lead, avoiding over-eager timing where the tighter route proved more reliable.</span></div>
  <div class="kg-card teal"><div class="eyebrow">D · Execution and closeout guards</div><b>Protect the plan from local failures.</b><br><span>Weed repair, feed and room guards, market-slot ranking, narrow opponent counters, and final liquidation keep operational errors from erasing the route's long-horizon value.</span></div>
</div>

<div class="kg-note gold"><div class="title">The key idea is bounded adaptation</div><div class="body">The controller does not turn market timing into a second full planner. It shifts only premium sells that are already scheduled by the route and later repays that shift by reducing the original sale. This keeps the intervention small, interpretable, and operationally coherent.</div></div>

### Choose how far to lead the premium sale

<div class="kg-prose"><div class="lead">The opening shop structure decides both the farm route and the sale-timing horizon. A first <b>YARN_STORE</b> or <b>BAKERY</b> keeps the one-turn premium lead. Other openings use a two-turn lead, giving the agent more queue priority when premium inventory is already waiting in the shed.</div></div>

<div class="kg-note violet"><div class="title">Why the rule stays simple</div><div class="body">The horizon is not optimized from a noisy spot quote every turn. It is tied to a persistent opening regime, while live inventory and route scheduling decide whether a particular premium sale can actually be shifted. That makes the market layer responsive without making the whole policy fragile.</div></div>

## 10. Mental model of one turn

<div class="kg-terminal">
live observation<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;↓<br>
read opening shops and select the matching full-season route<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;↓<br>
choose the premium lead horizon from the opening regime<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;↓<br>
read the route's farmer, hand, and market actions for this turn<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;↓<br>
repair a blocking weed or storage problem only when needed<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;↓<br>
rank planned sells by live market impact and town demand<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;↓<br>
if a premium sale is scheduled inside the lead window and inventory is ready, sell a bounded amount now<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;↓<br>
remember the shifted quantity and remove it from the original scheduled sale later<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;↓<br>
apply narrow opponent-response and feed guards<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;↓<br>
liquidate remaining sellable stock near the deadline<br>
&nbsp;&nbsp;↓<br>
return farmer + hands + market orders
</div>

<div class="kg-note"><div class="title">Reusable design principle</div><div class="body">When the environment has a shared market, the exact production plan and the exact selling time should be separate decisions. Commit to a coherent route, then use a compact timing layer to protect queue position without destabilizing labor and production.</div></div>
